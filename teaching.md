# Teaching

I teach master's courses in probability at TU Berlin. Course materials are linked below.
{: .lede}

## Courses at TU Berlin

- **Winter 2026/27.** [Advanced Probabilistic Methods Related to Reinforcement Learning](course-rl-2026.html). A mathematically rigorous treatment of bandits and Markov decision processes.
- **Summer 2026.** [Branching Processes](course-branching-2026.html). Bienaymé–Galton–Watson processes via generating functions, martingale limits and the Kesten–Stigum theorem, spine techniques, introduction to criticality and continuum random trees.
- **Winter 2025/26.** [Percolation](course-percolation-2025.html). The canonical probabilistic phase transition: from finite clusters to an infinite cluster.
- **Summer 2025.** [Topics in Branching Processes](course-branching-2025.html). Bienaymé–Galton–Watson processes and their long-run behaviour (the Kolmogorov and Yaglom limits, Kesten–Stigum and Seneta–Heyde).

## Student feedback

I ask students for anonymous feedback during each course and, for oral exams, afterwards too, and I adjust the course in response. Some comments:

- “The exam tested our understanding of the topics and not only our ability to memorise.”
- “The course helped me find a branch of maths which is fascinating.”
- “The quick sketches to illustrate something are really helpful.”

## Earlier teaching

At the University of Birmingham I taught probability and statistics, differential equations, algebra and combinatorics, numerical methods and programming, and Mathematics in Industry, and worked in the mathematics support centre. Before that I taught calculus and linear algebra at the Australian National University. I am an Associate Fellow of the Higher Education Academy.
