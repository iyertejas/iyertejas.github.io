1. For each fixed \(\lambda \in [0, \Lambda_m]\), if the prices are i.i.d. with mean at least \(m\), then \(\mathbb P(W_s(\lambda, m) \ge x \text{ for some } s) \le 1/x\) for every \(x > 0\).

True. \((W_s(\lambda, m))_{s \ge 0}\) starts at \(1\), is nonnegative since \(\lambda \le \Lambda_m\), and each factor has mean \(1 + \lambda(m - \mathrm{mean}(\mu)) \le 1\), so it is a nonnegative supermartingale, and Lemma 5.5 applies. For the best stake in hindsight, chosen after seeing the prices, this fails; Lecture 6 deals with it.

2. For rewards in \([0, 1]\), the best stake in Lemma 5.4 is always to go all in, \(\lambda^* = \Lambda_m\).

False. For a deterministic arm paying \(0.5\) and \(m = 0.6\), all in is best. For \(\mathrm{Ber}(0.5)\) the best stake is \(\frac{5}{12} < \Lambda_m = 2.5\): all in, the first reward \(1\) would wipe out the wealth, and \(\varphi(\Lambda_m) = -\infty\).

3. A deterministic arm paying \(p < m^*\) must be pulled at least of order \(\log n/\Delta^2\) times by every consistent policy over \(\mathcal M_{[0,1]}^K\).

False. Here \(d_a = K_{\inf}(\delta_p, m^*) = \log((1 - p)/(1 - m^*))\) (Remark 5.3 and Exercise 5.2), which is of order \(\Delta\), so the lower bound is of order \(\log n/\Delta\).

4. Ville's inequality bounds \(\mathbb P(M_n \ge x)\) by \(1/x\) for each fixed \(n\), but says nothing about all \(n\) at once.

False. For one fixed \(n\) the bound is Markov's inequality. Ville's inequality bounds the probability that \(M_n \ge x\) for some \(n\), that is, for all \(n\) simultaneously, by the same \(1/x\).

5. Knowing that the arms are Bernoulli gives a smaller Lai–Robbins constant than knowing only that the rewards lie in \([0, 1]\).

False. For Bernoulli arms both constants equal \(\mathrm{kl}(m_a, m^*)\) (Exercise 5.2(b)): the closest alternative on \([0, 1]\) is itself Bernoulli, as in the table of Section 3.

6. If the prices have mean below \(m\), the short seller's wealth grows for every stake \(\lambda \in (0, \Lambda_m]\).

False. Its logarithm grows at rate \(\varphi(\lambda)\), which is positive for small \(\lambda\) but can be negative: for \(\mathrm{Ber}(0.5)\) and \(m = 0.6\), \(\varphi(1) \approx -0.02\), and \(\varphi(\Lambda_m) = -\infty\). Try it in the simulation of Section 2.
