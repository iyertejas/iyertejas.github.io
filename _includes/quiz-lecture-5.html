1. For each fixed \(\lambda\), \((W_s(\lambda, m))_{s \ge 0}\) is a nonnegative supermartingale when the prices have mean at least \(m\), so by Ville's inequality \(\mathbb P(B_s(m) \ge x \text{ for some } s) \le 1/x\).

False. \(B_s(m)\) uses the stake that is best for the prices already seen, and it is not a supermartingale: already \(\mathbb E[B_1(m)] > 1\) (Exercise 5.3). Averaging over the stakes (Lemma 5.6) repairs this at the price of a factor \(s + 1\) (Corollary 5.7).

2. For rewards in \([0, 1]\), the best stake in Lemma 5.4 is always to go all in, \(\lambda^* = \Lambda_m\).

False. For a deterministic arm paying \(0.5\) and \(m = 0.6\), all in is best. For \(\mathrm{Ber}(0.5)\) the best stake is \(\frac{5}{12} < \Lambda_m = 2.5\): all in, the first reward \(1\) would wipe out the wealth, and \(\varphi(\Lambda_m) = -\infty\).

3. A deterministic arm paying \(p < m^*\) must be pulled at least of order \(\log n/\Delta^2\) times by every consistent policy over \(\mathcal M_{[0,1]}^K\).

False. Here \(d_a = K_{\inf}(\delta_p, m^*) = \log((1 - p)/(1 - m^*))\) (Remark 5.3 and Exercise 5.2), which is of order \(\Delta\), so the lower bound is of order \(\log n/\Delta\).

4. The K_inf index needs to know the variance of the rewards, or at least a bound on it.

False. It needs only that the rewards lie in \([0, 1]\). Since \(B_s(m) = \exp(s K_{\inf}(\hat\mu_s, m))\), it adapts to the whole empirical distribution of each arm, and so to its variance.

5. Knowing that the arms are Bernoulli gives a smaller Lai–Robbins constant than knowing only that the rewards lie in \([0, 1]\).

False. For Bernoulli arms both constants equal \(\mathrm{kl}(m_a, m^*)\) (Exercise 5.2(b)): the closest alternative on \([0, 1]\) is itself Bernoulli, as in the table of Section 3.

6. Corollary 5.7 holds for all sample sizes simultaneously.

True. This is Ville's inequality. So the random number of pulls of an arm costs nothing, and the price of the bound is the factor \(s + 1\) from Lemma 5.6, not a union bound over \(s \le n\).
