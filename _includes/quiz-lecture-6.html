1. If the prices are i.i.d. with mean at least \(m\), then the best wealth in hindsight \((B_s(m))_{s \ge 0}\) is a nonnegative supermartingale.

False. The stake is chosen after seeing the prices. For one price \(X \sim \mathrm{Ber}(m)\), the best stake is \(\Lambda_m\) if \(X = 0\) and \(0\) if \(X = 1\), so \(\mathbb E[B_1(m)] = (1 - m)\Lambda_m + m = 1 + m > 1 = B_0(m)\). This is why the bets are mixed.

2. If the prices are i.i.d. with mean at least \(m\), then the average \(M_s = \Lambda_m^{-1}\int_0^{\Lambda_m} W_s(\lambda, m)\, d\lambda\) is a nonnegative supermartingale.

True. Each \((W_s(\lambda, m))_{s \ge 0}\) is a nonnegative supermartingale, and the average is taken with weights that do not depend on the prices, so by Fubini's theorem \(M_s\) is one too, with \(M_0 = 1\). This is the proof of Corollary 6.2.

3. The factor \(s + 1\) in the threshold \((s + 1)n\) of Algorithm 6.3 pays for choosing the stake in hindsight.

True. By Lemma 6.1, the mixture \(M_s\) is at least \(B_s(m)/(s + 1)\), and Ville's inequality controls \(M_s\) at all \(s\) at once. The factor \(n\) sets the confidence level: a true mean is ever rejected with probability at most \(1/n\).

4. The index \(U_a(t-1)\) depends on the rewards of arm \(a\) only through their empirical mean and their number.

False. \(\log B_{s,a}(m) = s\, K_{\inf}(\hat\mu_{a,s}, m)\) depends on the whole empirical distribution. An arm that has paid \(0.5\) ten times has a much smaller index than one that has paid \(0\) and \(1\) five times each: the first is far stronger evidence that the mean is close to \(0.5\).

5. The Kinf index policy is asymptotically optimal for Bernoulli arms, although it is not told that the arms are Bernoulli.

True. By Theorem 6.4 it pulls arm \(a\) at most about \(\log n/K_{\inf}(\mathrm{Ber}(m_a), m^*)\) times, and \(K_{\inf}(\mathrm{Ber}(m_a), m^*) = \mathrm{kl}(m_a, m^*)\) (Exercise 5.2(b)), the Lai–Robbins constant for Bernoulli arms.

6. In the proof of Theorem 6.4, the stake \((1 - \varepsilon)\lambda^*\) is used instead of the Kelly stake \(\lambda^*\) because \(\lambda^*\) may be \(0\).

False. \(\varphi(\lambda^*) = K_{\inf}(\mu_a, m^*) > 0 = \varphi(0)\), so \(\lambda^* > 0\). The reason is that \(\lambda^*\) may equal \(\Lambda_{m^*}\), and then \(\log(1 + \lambda^*(m^* - Y))\) is unbounded below as \(Y \to 1\), so the Chernoff bound does not apply. With \((1 - \varepsilon)\lambda^*\) the logarithms lie in \([\log\varepsilon, \log\Lambda_{m^*}]\), and by concavity the growth rate drops only by a factor \(1 - \varepsilon\).
