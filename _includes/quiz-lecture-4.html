1. Theorem 4.1 applies to every policy, for example to the policy that always plays arm 1.

False. It applies only to policies that are consistent over \(\mathcal M^K\), and always playing arm 1 is not (Example 3.8). In an environment where arm 1 is the unique optimal arm, that policy has \(\mathbb E_\nu[N_a(n)] = 0\) for every \(a \ne 1\), so the bound of Theorem 4.1 fails there whenever \(d_a < \infty\). The proof uses consistency in \(\nu'\), the environment in which arm \(a\) is made optimal.

2. In the proof of Theorem 4.4, the arm whose law is changed can be fixed in advance, say arm 2.

False. It is an arm \(a' \ge 2\) with \(\mathbb E_\nu[N_{a'}(n)] \le n/(K-1)\), and which arm this is depends on \(\pi\). A policy that pulls arm 2 often would notice a change there: \(\mathbb E_\nu[N_2(n)]\,\mathrm{KL}\) could be of order \(n\).

3. In the proof of Theorem 4.4, the two environments depend on the horizon \(n\).

True. The changed arm \(a'\) is chosen using \(\mathbb E_\nu[N_{a'}(n)]\), so \(\nu'\) depends on \(n\) (and on \(\pi\)). The \(\Delta\) that makes the bound large, as in Corollary 4.5, depends on \(n\) too. For each \(n\), \(\nu\) and \(\nu'\) are still ordinary SBMs, fixed for the whole run.

4. \(d_a \ge d^{\Delta_a}\) for every arm \(a\) with \(\Delta_a > 0\).

True. \(d_a\) is the same infimum as \(d^{\Delta_a}\) (Definition 4.3), with \(\mu = \mu_a\) fixed instead of free. It can be several times larger (Exercise 4.1(c)).

5. In the proof of Theorem 4.4, the assumption that the means of \(\mathcal M\) form an interval is needed so that \(\nu' \in \mathcal M^K\).

False. \(\nu'\) is in \(\mathcal M^K\) because \(\mu' \in \mathcal M\). The assumption is used to find \(\mu_1 \in \mathcal M\) with mean exactly \(\mathrm{mean}(\mu_0) + \Delta\). Then every arm \(a \ge 2\) has gap \(\Delta\) in \(\nu\), and every arm other than \(a'\) has gap more than \(\Delta\) in \(\nu'\).

6. To use Lemma 4.7, the policy must know the threshold \(\eta\).

False. \(\eta\) appears only in the proof. The lemma turns any pull-count bound of the form \(A + B/\Delta_a^2\) into a worst-case bound.

7. If the means of \(\mathcal M\) form an interval, then \(R_n^*(\mathcal M^K)\) grows at least like \(\sqrt n\).

False. Theorem 4.4 also needs \(d^{2\Delta} < \infty\). For the uniform class \(\{\mathrm{Uniform}[m, m+1] : m \in [0,1]\}\), every \(d^\eta\) is infinite, and some policy has regret at most \(5(K-1)\) for every \(n\) (Exercise 4.3).
