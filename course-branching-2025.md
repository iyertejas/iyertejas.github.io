## Description

Branching processes are a natural model for the growth of a population over time, and are important in biology and other areas; perhaps the best-known application is epidemic modelling. The course covers the classical discrete-time Bienaymé–Galton–Watson process and, time permitting, processes with immigration and age-dependent processes:

- Generating functions and classical extinction criteria.
- Asymptotic theory for subcritical, critical and supercritical processes: the Kolmogorov and Yaglom limits, the Kesten–Stigum theorem and Seneta–Heyde norming.
- Continuous-time branching processes and age-dependent processes.
- Computational methods: simulations and modelling applications such as epidemiological spread.

## Prerequisites

Officially Probability I and II. Basic knowledge of Markov chains, conditional expectations and martingales (including the martingale convergence theorem) is enough to follow the course.

## Learning outcomes

On satisfying the requirements of this course, students will have a deeper understanding of branching processes and their connections to applications and other areas of probability. Students will be well-equipped to:

- Explain fundamental concepts concerning branching processes, and their role in applied contexts.
- Apply branching-process arguments, as well as more fundamental probabilistic techniques such as martingales, in a variety of problem-solving contexts.
